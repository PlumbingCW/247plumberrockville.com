Plumbing in College Gardens and Woodley Gardens

According to the City's plan, Planning Area 5 is primarily the Woodley Gardens and College Gardens neighborhoods, with I-270 to the west and Upper Watts Branch Park to the east. Residential development began in the early 1960s, and most blocks were built between the mid-1960s and early 1970s, with some northern lots following in the early 1980s. The December 2025 report counts 1,549 homes and about 3,988 residents.

What the City records say about this housing mix

In 2019 the area had 632 detached homes, 641 attached homes and 272 apartment or condominium units. Detached houses sit on their own lots, while townhomes and apartments sit on common parcels held by homeowners associations and commercial owners.

Named communities include Plymouth Woods, Regents Square, Scarborough Square, Woodley Gardens Co-op and Yale Village.

Source type: City of Rockville 2040 Comprehensive Plan, Planning Area 5 (unit counts from 2019 City land records).

A near-even split between houses and townhomes

With roughly as many attached homes as detached ones, this is an area where ownership details matter. A detached house owner is responsible for the pipes on the lot, while a townhome owner may share walls, a roof and sometimes service lines with neighbors. Before authorizing a repair, find out where your responsibility ends. Your association documents usually spell it out, and a plumber can help you identify which shutoff controls which part of the house.

Water, grading and the stream valley

Upper Watts Branch Park is a 79-acre forested stream valley that runs through the area, and College Gardens Park includes a landscaped stormwater pond that the City renovated as a stormwater retrofit.

Homes near stream valleys and low points can see more groundwater after heavy rain, though the plan does not say which houses. If a basement takes on water after storms, a sump pump that works and a discharge that runs away from the foundation are the first things to check.

A sump pit that fills faster than the pump empties it, a pump that runs without stopping or one that has not been tested in a year are all reasons to call. Not verified here: which addresses see groundwater, since the City's plan does not list them. Persistent dampness can be a drainage matter beyond plumbing, and basement waterproofing explains where plumbing ends and exterior work begins.

Licenses in Maryland come from the state Board of Plumbing, whose public search anyone can use. If your association approves vendors, give them the plumber's license details, and ask the plumber to explain the repair in plain terms before work starts.
