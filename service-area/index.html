Rockville 24/7 Plumbing covers the City of Rockville, its neighborhoods and five nearby Montgomery County communities. Each nearby town has its own page with ZIP codes, local housing notes and what to prepare before a plumber arrives.

Rockville and its neighborhoods

Rockville is the county seat of Montgomery County and was incorporated in 1860. It has more than 67,000 residents and a mix of housing, from West End Park, laid out around 1890, and Lincoln Park, founded in 1891, to postwar Twinbrook and the planned communities of King Farm and Fallsgrove. Because house age varies street by street, we ask when the house was built before assuming anything about its pipes.

Rockville's water comes from the City for most addresses and from WSSC Water for the rest, so check your bill to see which one serves you. Our drain cleaning, leak detection and water heater replacement pages are good places to start.

The five nearby towns

Gaithersburg is a city of about 69,000 north of Rockville, with housing from its fast-growth decades after 1970. with a mix of apartments, condos and single-family houses.

Potomac has larger homes and longer pipe runs, and some properties use wells and septic. Derwood sits between Rockville and Gaithersburg, and Aspen Hill fills the gap between Rockville and Wheaton with mid-century ramblers.

Neighborhoods inside Rockville

Within the city, seven neighborhood pages use the City's own planning records: Twinbrook, King Farm, West End, East Rockville, College Gardens and Woodley Gardens, Hungerford and New Mark Commons and Rockshire and Fallsmead.

What to have ready when you call

A street address, the nearest cross street and a callback number are the three most useful details. If you live in a condo or townhome, tell us the building and unit and whether there is a gate or a parking limit. If the problem is in a shared wall or a shared stack, note who manages the building.

If you can, find the main water shutoff before the plumber arrives. We will talk you through it on the phone if you cannot find it, and the plumber will check that it works.

Why where you live matters to plumbing

Water supply differs across our area. The City of Rockville treats and distributes water for roughly 70 percent of the city, and WSSC Water serves the rest of Rockville and the surrounding Montgomery County communities. Your water bill shows which utility serves your address.

That matters for leaks, bills and sewer backups, because the utility owns the meter and the main while the owner is responsible for the pipes on the property. WSSC Water is also the plumbing inspection agency for the county, so permit questions go through them. We explain where the line falls on your property when we visit.

Coverage is a matter of distance and timing, not a hard line on a map. If your address is close to one of the towns above, call and tell us where you are. We will say honestly whether we can help quickly, and if another plumber is a better choice for an urgent problem, we will say that too.

Whichever town you call from, the process is the same. Call to ask what help is available for your address.
