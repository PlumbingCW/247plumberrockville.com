What counts as a plumbing emergency

An emergency plumber is worth calling when water is doing damage or a health risk is building. Water pouring from a pipe, sewage coming up a floor drain, a water heater leaking across the basement and a smell of gas all qualify, and none of them should wait for office hours.

Smaller problems can often wait for a regular appointment, but plenty of people call anyway to ask, and that is fine. Describing what you see helps work out whether it can wait and what to do in the meantime.

When you call, expect questions about where the water is, whether you can reach the main shutoff and whether anyone is in danger. Ask about timing for your address, and ask for the price before any work starts.

Where we work

Rockville and the towns around it

We cover Rockville, the county seat of Montgomery County, with more than 67,000 residents. The City's own plan describes a long mix of housing, from West End Park, laid out around 1890, to postwar Twinbrook and the planned communities of King Farm and Fallsgrove, so pipe age varies from street to street.

What that means for you is that the right fix depends on the house, not the neighborhood name. A camera inspection shows what a drain line is made of and what shape it is in, and a newer townhome is more likely to have a fixture or valve problem.

Rockville water in brief

The City of Rockville treats and distributes its own drinking water from the Potomac River for roughly 70 percent of the city, and WSSC Water serves the rest. According to the City's most recent water quality report, lead tested below 1 part per billion at the 90th percentile. The report does not list a hardness figure, so ask your utility or test your tap if you are deciding about a softener.

WSSC Water is also the plumbing inspection agency in Montgomery County, with its own plumbing code, so permit steps for larger jobs go through them and we confirm what applies before we start.

Emergency plumber questions in Rockville

Call (301) 936-0389 and describe the problem, such as a leak, a burst pipe, a sewer backup or a failed water heater. Ask what help and timing are possible for your address, because they depend on the situation. If there is a gas smell, a fire or an electrical hazard, leave the building and call 911 first.

Close the main water shutoff if water is leaking, stop using drains if they are backing up, switch off the water heater if it is leaking and keep everyone away from standing water near electrical outlets.
