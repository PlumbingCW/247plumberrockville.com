Installing a New Garbage Disposal in Rockville

A garbage disposal is one of the smaller plumbing jobs in a kitchen, and also one of the easiest to get wrong. This guide covers sizing, the install steps and the mistakes that cause leaks.

If your disposal is jammed or leaking right now, switch it off at the wall and call a plumber.

Picking the right disposal

Motor size is the first choice. A one-third horsepower unit handles light use. A half to three-quarter horsepower unit suits a typical family kitchen. A one horsepower model is for heavy use and is quieter, since it has more insulation. Continuous feed units run while the switch is on. Batch feed units run only with the lid in place.

SizeBest forNotes
One-third horsepowerLight use, small householdsLowest cost, jams more easily.
One-half horsepowerMost householdsGood balance.
Three-quarter horsepowerBusy kitchensBetter grinding.
One horsepowerHeavy useQuietest, most expensive.

A disposal is only as good as the drain behind it. If the sink drains slowly now, the new unit will not fix that and may make it worse. Read about drain cleaning first if your kitchen line is already sluggish.

How the installation goes

  1. Switch off power at the breaker and confirm the old unit is dead.
  2. Disconnect the drain trap and the dishwasher hose, then remove the old unit from its mount.
  3. Set the new sink flange with plumber's putty and tighten it from below.
  4. Mount the new unit on the flange, connect the discharge tube and the dishwasher knockout, if the dishwasher drains through it.
  5. Reconnect the trap, wire the unit and run water to test for leaks.

Two steps cause most problems. One is forgetting to knock out the plug in the dishwasher inlet when a dishwasher connects, which backs the dishwasher up. The other is an overtightened or unsealed flange, which drips into the cabinet and damages the base over time.

Wiring and power

Disposals are wired directly or plugged into an outlet under the sink, depending on the model and the kitchen. The circuit has to carry the motor's load, and the switch has to be placed where it is easy to reach and out of children's way. A switch that is hard to find means people reach into the unit to check it, which is a risk.

If the wall switch is missing, an air switch on the countertop is a safe alternative that does not need a new wire through the wall. Hire an electrician if the circuit is not up to the job. A plumber can handle the water side and the disposal itself.

  • Confirm the breaker size matches the disposal's rating.
  • Make sure the outlet is grounded.
  • Place the switch away from the sink edge.
  • Keep the power off until every connection is checked.

Tools and parts you will need

A typical installation needs a screwdriver, pliers, a hex key, plumber's putty, a bucket and a new trap kit if the old one is worn. A mounting kit is often included with the disposal, but check that it fits your sink. If your sink is stainless steel, the flange seats easily, and if it is a thin composite, extra care is needed to avoid cracking it.

Testing after the install

Run cold water into the sink, switch the disposal on briefly with no food and check for leaks at the flange, the discharge tube and the trap. Look inside the cabinet with a flashlight after a few minutes. Then grind a small load of soft scraps and check again, since vibration can loosen a clamp.

Habits that keep a disposal healthy

  • Run cold water before, during and for a short time after grinding.
  • Feed scraps in slowly, not in a pile.
  • Keep grease, coffee grounds, bones and fibrous stalks out.
  • Clean with ice and citrus rinds now and then, not with caustic cleaners.

Grease is the repeat offender. It cools in pipes beyond the disposal and builds a coating that catches everything else. If your kitchen line fills with grease, hydro jetting can scour the pipe wall.

Where kitchen waste goes in Rockville

Most Rockville addresses connect to the public sewer. In WSSC Water territory the owner is responsible for the sewer pipe from the property line to the building, while WSSC maintains the main. Grease from the kitchen builds up in the owner's section first.

Some properties near the edge of the service area use private septic systems, which Montgomery County says owners maintain and whose performance depends on soil and rock. Ask a plumber before installing a disposal on a septic system, since extra solids affect the tank.

Source type: WSSC Water sewer responsibility guidance and Montgomery County individual system guidance
