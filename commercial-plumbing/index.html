When a commercial plumbing problem is an emergency

A restaurant with a backed-up grease line cannot serve food. An office with a flooded restroom loses a floor. A retail suite with a burst pipe damages tenants' stock and neighboring spaces. In each case, the first job is stopping the water and the second is limiting the downtime.

Know where your building's shutoffs are, both for the suite and for the whole building, and keep the landlord or property manager's contact handy. Our guide to burst pipe repair covers the emergency steps, and for grease-driven backups see hydro jetting.

While you wait

  • Close the shutoff for the affected suite or restroom.
  • Put out wet floor signs and keep people away.
  • Photograph damage for the landlord and insurer.
  • Tell the property manager and note who is responsible for which pipes.

Planning ahead for a business

The best time to find a plumber is before a failure. Keep our number saved, label your shutoffs and note which pipes belong to you under the lease. A short walkthrough can also reveal aging valves and drains that are likely to fail.

  • Know where the water, gas and floor drain cleanouts are.
  • Schedule grease line cleaning before it backs up, not after.
  • Keep records of backflow testing dates.
  • Tell staff who to call and what to shut off first.

Landlords, tenants and who calls us

In a multi-tenant building, the first question is whose pipe it is. Usually the tenant handles fixtures and branch lines inside the suite, and the landlord handles risers, mains and shared drains. A call to the property manager first can save a dispute later, and we can document the source so the right party pays.

If the problem threatens neighboring suites, shut off what you can reach and call us at once. Water travels through shared walls faster than most people expect.
