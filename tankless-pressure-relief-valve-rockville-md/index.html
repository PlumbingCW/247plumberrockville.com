Tankless Water Heater Pressure Relief Valves in Rockville

A pressure relief valve is a safety device that opens when pressure or temperature gets too high. If you have a tankless heater and a valve that drips, this guide explains why and what to do.

If the valve is spraying hot water, shut off power or gas to the heater and call a plumber right away.

What the relief valve does

A temperature and pressure relief valve, often called a T&P valve, is the last line of defense on a water heater. If pressure or temperature rises past a safe level, the valve opens and lets water escape. It should never be capped, plugged or removed.

Tank heaters nearly always have one. Tankless units hold far less water, so the design differs, and some have a pressure relief valve on the water side while others depend on the house system. Your manual and the label on the unit say what yours has. If anything is unclear, ask a plumber rather than guess.

Why a valve drips

CauseWhat happensFix
Thermal expansionHeated water expands in a closed system and pressure spikesAdd or service an expansion tank.
High supply pressureStreet or house pressure exceeds the valve's settingInstall or adjust a pressure reducing valve.
Worn valve seatValve no longer seals after years of useReplace the valve.
Mineral buildupScale keeps the valve slightly openReplace the valve.
OverheatingControls fail and temperature climbsShut off and call a plumber.

A slow drip after a hot water draw is often thermal expansion. A constant stream is a warning that something is wrong. Keep the discharge pipe open and pointed downward, and never close it off to stop a drip.

Testing the valve safely

Many manufacturers suggest lifting the valve lever briefly once a year to see that it moves and that water flows from the discharge pipe. Do this only if the manual says it is safe for your model, keep a bucket under the pipe and expect hot water. If the valve does not reseat and keeps dripping, it needs replacing.

Never test a valve by holding it open for a long time, and never use a tool to force it. Pressure relief valves are designed to last a long while, but they wear out, especially in homes with high water pressure or heavy mineral content.

  • Keep the discharge pipe open, pointing down and not threaded at the end.
  • Replace a valve that leaks after being tested.
  • Install an expansion tank if the house has a check valve or a pressure reducing valve.
  • Have pressure checked whenever a relief valve starts to drip.

Replacing a relief valve

Replacement is a short job for a plumber. The water is shut off, the heater is drained to a level below the valve, the old valve is removed and a new one of the same rating is installed, with the discharge pipe reconnected. Never swap in a valve with a different pressure or temperature rating, and never plug the opening to stop a leak.

Expansion tanks and pressure reducing valves

Many houses have a backflow preventer or a pressure reducing valve on the incoming line, which turns the plumbing into a closed system. Water has nowhere to expand, so pressure rises each time the heater runs. A small expansion tank on the cold line absorbs that growth and spares the relief valve.

A pressure reducing valve brings street pressure down to a safe level for the house. When one fails, pressure climbs, fixtures wear out faster and relief valves start to weep. We test pressure with a gauge before suggesting parts.

What you can safely check

  • Look for moisture at the end of the discharge pipe.
  • Note whether the drip happens only after hot water use.
  • Check the discharge pipe for kinks or caps.
  • Do not twist or pry the valve lever repeatedly, which can leave it leaking.

Pressure and your water supplier

Water pressure at your house depends on your supplier and your location. In Rockville the City treats and distributes water to about 70 percent of the city, and WSSC Water serves the rest, so check your bill to see who supplies you.

We do not quote a standard pressure for Rockville. A gauge on an outdoor hose bib gives you a real reading, and a plumber can read it with you. For safety work on the heater, WSSC Water is the plumbing inspection agency for Montgomery County.
