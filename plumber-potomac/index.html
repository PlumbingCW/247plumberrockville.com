Long pipe runs and hidden failures

A longer house means longer pipes and more joints. A slow leak in a wall behind a bathroom can run for weeks before a stain appears. If your water bill jumps or you hear running water, leak detection starts with a meter test and narrows from there.

Larger lots often have long service lines from the road, and a failure there floods a yard or lowers pressure. See water service line repair for how those jobs work.

Wells, septic and the public system

Parts of Montgomery County, especially outside the public water and sewer service area, use private wells and septic systems. The county notes that owners maintain these systems and that performance depends on soil and rock. If your Potomac property is on one, tell us when you call, because it changes what we check.

Large homes and multiple systems

A big house often has two or three water heaters, several bathrooms and a long hot water loop. When one fails, it can mean cold showers upstairs and normal water downstairs, which confuses owners. A recirculating pump, a mixing valve or a zone valve may be the real culprit and not the heater itself.

  • Multiple water heaters that serve different wings or floors.
  • Recirculation pumps that fail and leave long waits for hot water.
  • Irrigation lines that tie into the house supply.
  • Finished lower levels where a leak can run unseen.

We trace each system separately and explain the layout, so you know what you have. For a failing unit, our water heater replacement page covers the options, and a hunt for a wet spot starts with leak detection.

Access and scheduling

Potomac properties often have long driveways, gates and detached garages. Tell us if there is a code, a dog or a steep driveway when you call. If your house has a basement apartment or a guest wing with its own shutoff, mention that, since it changes where the plumber starts. In an emergency, leave a door open or have someone meet us, and we will be ready.

Large homes also tend to have more outdoor spigots and irrigation tie-ins, so a winter freeze can strike far from the main. Closing and draining outdoor lines in autumn is a small chore that prevents a large repair.

Where this comes from. According to U.S. Census figures, Potomac is an unincorporated community of about 47,000 people. Montgomery County states that private wells and septic systems are maintained by the owner.
