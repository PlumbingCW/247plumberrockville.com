When a toilet problem is an emergency

An overflowing bowl is an emergency because the water is not clean. A leak at the base can rot the subfloor over time and drip into the ceiling below. A toilet that will not stop running wastes water all day and can add a lot to your bill.

If more than one fixture is backing up, the cause may be the main drain and not the toilet, and our drain cleaning page explains what to do. If the toilet alone is clogged, a plunger often works, and if it does not, call us.

Stop the water first

  • Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise.
  • Lift the tank lid and lift the float to stop refilling.
  • Do not flush again.
  • Put towels around the base and keep children away from the water.

Reading a running toilet

A toilet that runs on its own usually has a flapper that no longer seals, a fill valve that does not shut off or a float set too high. The easiest test is food coloring in the tank, which shows if water is leaking into the bowl.

Parts for these repairs are inexpensive and widely available. The labor is quick when the shutoff valve works, and slower if it has seized, which is why we check it first.

When to replace the toilet

If the porcelain is cracked, if the bowl will not clear even after several repairs or if the toilet is several decades old and wastes water, replacement is the better value. A new toilet fits the existing flange and shutoff in most cases, so the job is often quick.

Toilets in Rockville homes

Older toilets may have older flush valves and rigid supply lines. Replacing the fill valve and flapper is inexpensive, but a toilet that is 25 years old may be a better candidate for replacement, which saves water and ends repeat repairs.

If a toilet clogs repeatedly while other fixtures drain fine, the trap or a nearby bend is likely partly blocked. A look with a camera, described under sewer inspection, shows whether a deeper fault exists. For faucets and sinks see faucet repair, and for neighbors outside the city see our Derwood page.

Where this comes from. According to WSSC Water's sewer backup guidance, the owner is responsible for the pipe from the property line to the building, and WSSC maintains its main and the connection. If several fixtures back up, the problem may be in that line and not the toilet.
