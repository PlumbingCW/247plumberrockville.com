Read Your Water Meter and Test for a Leak in Rockville

Your water meter is the most honest leak detector in the house. It counts every gallon that passes, so if it moves while everything is off, water is escaping somewhere.

This guide shows how to read the meter, run a simple test and narrow down where the leak might be.

Finding and reading the meter

Most meters sit in a box near the street or in a basement or utility area where the water line enters. Lift the lid carefully, since insects like these boxes. The face shows a register of numbers like an odometer, and many meters also have a small triangle or wheel, the low-flow indicator, that spins when any water moves.

Photograph the numbers and the indicator. If your meter has a unit label, such as cubic feet or gallons, note it, because your bill will use the same unit.

Run the leak test

  • Turn off every faucet, the dishwasher, washing machine, ice maker and irrigation system.
  • Look at the low-flow indicator. If it spins or moves, water is flowing.
  • If it is still, write the register reading and wait 30 minutes with no water use.
  • Read it again. Any change means a leak somewhere past the meter.

Reading the numbers and units

Meter registers count either cubic feet or gallons, and a multiplier may apply. If the face says 100 cubic feet per unit, the last digit shows a larger quantity than you might expect, and a sweep hand or a small wheel often shows the tiny amounts. Check the label on the meter and compare it with your latest bill to get the units right.

If you use a smartphone camera, take a clear close-up before and after. Photos beat memory, especially if you need to show your utility or an insurer that the meter moved while the house was idle. Date the photos or keep them in a folder with the test date.

  • Note the units and multiplier on the meter face.
  • Photograph both the register and the low-flow wheel.
  • Keep the test photos with your bill.
  • Repeat after any repair to confirm the leak is gone.

After you find a leak

Once you know a leak exists, stop the loss. Close the main if water is visibly escaping, or close the fixture stop for a toilet or sink if you found the source. Photograph the meter and any wet area. Then call a plumber, so the exact source is located and repaired, not just guessed at.

Seasonal leaks to watch for

Some leaks come and go with the season. An irrigation valve that sticks open in spring, a hose bib that drips after a freeze or a humidifier line that fails in winter can all show up on a meter. Run the test again after you turn irrigation on or off, and after any cold snap, since conditions change.

A toilet dye test

Toilets are the most common silent leak. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing and look in the bowl. If color appears, the flapper is leaking into the bowl. A worn flapper is cheap to replace and can waste a surprising amount of water.

Rockville meters and billing

Most Rockville addresses are served by the City of Rockville, which owns the meters for its customers. WSSC Water serves the rest, so your bill tells you who reads yours. The City has an advanced metering project for City customers that is meant to flag unusual use and leaks automatically, but vendor selection is in progress and no dates are set, and WSSC customers are not part of it.

Until a smart meter reaches you, the manual test above is a reliable early warning. Ask your utility about leak adjustments and billing questions, since policies are theirs to state.
