When a sump pump failure is an emergency

A sump pump fails when you need it most, during a storm, when it runs for hours. A stuck float, a clogged intake, a failed motor or a power outage can all stop it. Once the pit overflows, the basement floods.

If water is already rising, call right away. If you have a working shop vac or a spare pump, use them to buy time. For ongoing seepage and moisture problems, read about basement waterproofing.

Check these while you wait

  • Make sure the pump is plugged in and the breaker is on.
  • Lift the float by hand to see whether the pump starts.
  • Look for a frozen or kinked discharge hose.
  • Never reach into the pit unless the power is off.

Maintenance that prevents failures

  • Lift the float or pour water in the pit each spring to test the pump.
  • Clean the pit of silt and gravel at least once a year.
  • Check that the discharge line is clear and not frozen or kinked.
  • Replace the backup battery on schedule.
  • Replace a pump that is approaching a decade of service.

These steps take minutes and cost little. Many sump pump failures happen in pumps that were never tested between storms.

Choosing a replacement pump

Most replacements are for a pump that has run for years and finally quit. A direct swap with the same horsepower and a quality check valve is often enough. If the pit has been overwhelmed, we may suggest a stronger pump, a second pit or a battery backup.

We also check the float switch type. A vertical float is simple and reliable, while a tethered float needs room to swing in a narrow pit and can hang up on the wall.

Where Rockville basements get wet

Rock Creek, Cabin John Creek and Watts Branch run through and around Rockville, and homes on low ground or near these valleys tend to see the most groundwater. Hard rain can also overwhelm older foundation drains, so a pump in an older house may work harder than one in a newer home.

Discharge matters too. Storm and groundwater are among the things WSSC bars from the sanitary sewer, so a pump should discharge outdoors and away from the foundation, and we check your lot's setup. Our leak detection service helps when the water source is not the pit, and our Potomac page covers larger lots with sloped yards.

Where this comes from. According to WSSC Water's list of prohibited discharges, storm water and ground water do not belong in the sanitary sewer, so discharge outdoors.
