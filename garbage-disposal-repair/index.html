When a disposal problem is an emergency

A leak that drips into the cabinet can ruin the base in a day. A backed-up sink full of dirty water is a sanitation problem. A humming motor that will not turn can overheat. Switch the unit off and use the wall switch and breaker so nothing starts unexpectedly.

If the backup also shows in the dishwasher or the second sink, the problem may be in the drain line and not the disposal, so read about drain cleaning too.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Turn off the wall switch and, if you can, the breaker.
  • Never put your hand into the disposal.
  • Use tongs or pliers to remove a visible object only after power is off.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit once the motor cools.

Repair or replace the disposal

A jam, a reset or a loose connection is a quick fix. A seized motor, a cracked housing or a leak from the grinding chamber means replacement. As a rule, a disposal that is more than a decade old and failing is not worth a major repair.

When we replace a unit, we also check the drain line, because a slow sink will clog the new disposal as well. Fixing both at once avoids a callback.

Odors and slow sinks

A bad smell from the sink is usually food residue on the grinding chamber and the underside of the rubber splash guard. Run cold water, grind ice and a few citrus peels, and scrub the splash guard with a brush.

If the smell comes back right away, the problem may be in the trap or the drain line beyond it. That is a drain cleaning visit, not a disposal repair, and it saves you from buying a new unit you did not need.

Keep the hex key that came with your unit, since it is the tool that frees most jams. Store it where you can find it quickly, because a jammed disposal rarely waits for a convenient time.

What goes down a kitchen drain in Rockville

Disposals are built for soft food scraps, not grease, coffee grounds, potato peels or bones. Grease cools and coats pipes downstream, which is a common reason kitchen lines clog.

If the disposal is old and the sink drains slowly, replacing both the unit and clearing the line is often cheaper than repairing the unit alone. A hydro jetting visit helps when grease is the repeat cause, and our North Bethesda page covers homes with similar kitchens.

Where this comes from. According to WSSC Water's sewer backup guidance, the owner is responsible for the pipe from the property line to the building, and WSSC maintains its main and the connection. Kitchen grease collects in your section first.
