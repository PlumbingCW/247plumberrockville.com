Plumbing in King Farm, Rockville

According to the City of Rockville, King Farm is the best-known part of Planning Area 16, which the City calls King Farm and Shady Grove. The community was built on a former 440-acre farm that the City annexed in 1995 and approved as a neo-traditional neighborhood, with construction beginning in early 2000. Build-out is described as generally complete. The December 2025 report counts 5,633 homes and about 10,804 residents for the planning area.

What the City records say about King Farm housing

By the City's 2019 count the planning area had 394 detached homes, 972 attached homes, 2,850 apartment or condominium units and 650 assisted living or nursing home units, 4,866 in all. More than half the units are in multi-unit buildings.

Named communities in the plan include the Huntington at King Farm, the Residences at King Farm and Ingleside at King Farm.

Source type: City of Rockville 2040 Comprehensive Plan, Planning Area 16 (unit counts from 2019 City land records).

Who is responsible for which pipe

The main plumbing question in a place like this is not what the pipes are made of. It is who owns the part that is leaking. In an apartment or condominium building, a shared stack, riser or main line usually belongs to the association or the building owner, while the lines and fixtures inside your unit belong to you. Townhomes on common parcels have their own split.

Read your governing documents or ask your management office before calling anyone to repair something that may not be yours.

This is general guidance, not a statement about any specific building. Rules differ by association, so ask yours which shutoff valves, water heaters and drain lines it maintains.

Common calls for townhome and condo owners

  • A water heater that leaks or stops heating, covered under water heater repair and water heater installation.
  • A toilet or fixture that runs or overflows, handled with toilet repair.
  • A drain that slows in one unit only, which points to a branch line and is a job for drain cleaning.
  • A stain on a ceiling or wall that suggests a leak above, where leak detection helps trace the source.

Because buildings share walls and floors, water travels. Close your unit's shutoff, tell the management office and take photos before cleanup, since the association and its insurer may need them.

Not verified here: the pipe material inside any specific King Farm building, or each association's rules. The City's plan describes the community's size, history and housing mix, not its internal plumbing, so the building's management office is the right source for shared lines.

Whoever you hire, the state Board of Plumbing, part of the Maryland Department of Labor, licenses plumbers and runs a public search tool. Management offices usually ask for proof of licensing before they allow work in a shared building, so look it up before the visit.
