When a clogged drain is an emergency

One slow sink is an annoyance. Two fixtures gurgling or backing up at the same time points to the main drain, and that is when water starts to rise in the lowest opening of the house, usually a basement shower or floor drain.

Skip the bottle of caustic cleaner. It sits in standing water, can burn skin when the plumber opens the trap, and rarely reaches a clog that is farther down the line. If the main line is involved, see sewer line cleaning for what comes next.

While you wait for the plumber

  • Stop using water in the house, including dishwashers and washing machines.
  • Do not flush the toilet if the tub or basement drain is gurgling.
  • Look for a cleanout cap near the foundation or in the basement, but do not open it under pressure.
  • Take a photo of any overflow for your records.

How we clear different kinds of clogs

A sink or tub clog is usually hair, soap and grease close to the fixture, and a small cable machine clears it. A kitchen line with years of cooking fat needs a heavier cutting head. A main line full of roots needs a larger machine and, often, a camera pass to see what is down there.

Warning signs before a full blockage

Drains usually complain before they quit. Gurgling after you flush, water that pools in the tub while you shower, a smell from the kitchen sink and a drain that clears and then slows again within a day are all signs of a partial clog.

Catching it at this stage keeps the cleaning small and cheap. Ignore it, and a partial clog grows into a backup, usually at the worst time, such as when guests are over or the washing machine is mid-cycle.

Rockville drains and why they clog

In older homes anywhere, some drain lines may be cast iron or clay, which can roughen with age and catch grease and roots, so a camera inspection shows what is actually there. A rough pipe wall is why the same drain can clog again within months.

In newer King Farm and Fallsgrove homes the pipes are smoother, and clogs are more often hair, wipes and kitchen grease. Either way, the fix starts with knowing which pipe is blocked, which is why we often pair cleaning with a short camera check. If a clog keeps returning, a longer look with sewer inspection shows what the pipe is doing. Homes of that age are covered on our North Bethesda page.

Habits that keep drains open

  • Wipe grease from pans into the trash before washing.
  • Use a hair catcher in showers and tubs.
  • Flush only toilet paper, not wipes sold as flushable.
  • Run hot water for 30 seconds after washing greasy dishes.

Where this comes from. According to WSSC Water's sewer backup guidance, the owner is responsible for the pipe from the property line to the building, and WSSC maintains its main and the connection. A clog on your side of that line is yours to clear.
