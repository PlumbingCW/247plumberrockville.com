Rockville 24/7 Plumbing is a phone line for plumbing help in Rockville, Maryland and the Montgomery County communities around it. Call and describe the problem, from a leaking faucet to a failed sewer line, and ask what help and timing are possible.

What this site is for

This site explains common plumbing problems in plain language and gives you one number to call. The guides and service pages describe what usually causes a problem, what you can safely do while you wait and what questions to ask. Whoever you hire, you should be able to get an explanation of the problem and a price before any repair begins.

We do not quote hardness numbers, code details or permit rules from memory. Those come from the utility and the inspection agency, and the pages tell you where to confirm them. In Montgomery County that agency is WSSC Water, which has its own plumbing and fuel gas code.

How a call works

  1. You call and describe what you see.
  2. You are told how to limit damage, such as shutting off water or power if it is safe.
  3. Ask about timing and availability for your address, since they depend on the situation.
  4. Ask for the price and what it covers before you agree to any work.
  5. Ask what caused the problem and how to avoid a repeat.

How the local pages are written

The neighborhood pages for places such as Twinbrook and King Farm rely on the City of Rockville's own planning records for facts like when an area was built and what kinds of homes it has. Where no official source exists, such as pipe materials on a given street, the pages say so instead of guessing.

We keep general plumbing guidance separate from sourced local facts, and we do not publish reviews, awards or credentials that we cannot show you. If a statement on any page looks wrong, email us and we will check it.

Why older Rockville homes need a different approach

Rockville has a long mix of housing ages, from Victorian-era houses in West End Park to planned communities like King Farm. A house of any age may still have older galvanized supply pipe or cast iron drains that have been repaired many times. A house from the last two decades is more likely to have copper or plastic, with fixture and valve issues instead.

That is why we ask about age at the start of every call. The same symptom, a slow drain or low pressure, can mean very different things in different houses, and the right fix follows from the right diagnosis.

Keeping our promises small and clear

We would rather make a small promise and keep it than a large one and miss. Call, describe the problem and ask for the price before work begins. We will not guarantee an arrival time, because traffic, weather and other emergencies make that dishonest.
