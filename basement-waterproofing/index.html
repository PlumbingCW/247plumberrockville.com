Find out where the water comes from

Not every wet basement is a waterproofing problem. A leaking supply pipe, a failed water heater or a cracked drain can mimic seepage. We start by ruling those out, because a sump pump will not fix a pipe that is leaking in a wall.

If the source is groundwater, the answer is usually to collect it and move it out. If it is a supply line, leak detection comes first.

Signs your basement needs attention

White chalky stains on walls, a musty smell, peeling paint on foundation walls, water at the wall-floor joint after rain and rust on appliance feet are all signs of moisture. Each one has a cause, and the cause decides the fix.

Taking notes helps. When did you see water, was it after rain or constant, and where did it appear? Those answers separate a plumbing leak from groundwater, and we bring them to the first visit.

Questions to ask any waterproofing contractor

  • Did you find where the water enters, or did you assume?
  • Will the system need a sump pump, and where will it discharge?
  • What happens to the water if the power fails?
  • What is covered, for how long, and who services it?

A contractor who cannot answer these is selling a package, not a solution. Ask for the answers in writing, and keep them with the house records.

What interior waterproofing involves

An interior drain system sits along the footing and carries water to a sump pit, where a pump discharges it outside. The pit needs a sealed lid, a check valve and, in many homes, a battery backup. Our sump pump repair page covers the pump itself.

Plumbers handle the pump, the discharge and the interior drain. Foundation cracks, exterior excavation and regrading are different trades, and we will say so if your problem falls there. We would rather refer you than sell the wrong job.

Why Rockville basements stay damp

Many Rockville neighborhoods sit near Rock Creek, Cabin John Creek and Watts Branch, so groundwater runs close to the surface in wet seasons. Older houses may have foundation drains that clog with silt over time. What this means for you is that a basement that stayed dry for decades can start leaking after a wet year.

Discharge goes outdoors and away from the foundation. Storm and groundwater are among the things WSSC prohibits in the sanitary sewer, so we do not connect a sump to the house drain. See our Aspen Hill page for neighborhoods with similar low spots.

Where this comes from. According to WSSC Water's list of prohibited discharges, storm water and ground water do not belong in the sanitary sewer. Montgomery County also advises residents of a municipality or HOA to check local rules.
