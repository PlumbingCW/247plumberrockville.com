What we install

New bathrooms, kitchens, laundry rooms and basement baths all start with rough-in plumbing, the supply and drain lines inside walls and floors. Done right, nothing is visible and nothing leaks. Done wrong, you open the wall again.

We also set fixtures, hook up dishwashers and ice makers, add shutoff valves and install outdoor faucets. For heater work see water heater installation, and for old pipe replacement see repiping.

Plan plumbing before finishing work

The best time to involve a plumber is before the tile is ordered. Fixture locations, drain slopes and shutoff access are easy to set when walls are open and painful to change later. A short visit before a remodel can save weeks.

Bring the fixture specifications, since rough-in dimensions vary by model. Confirm the details with the utility and the inspection agency before work begins.

Installing a new dishwasher or ice maker

Appliance hookups look easy and often fail slowly. A dishwasher needs a dedicated supply stop, a drain connected above the trap with a high loop or an air gap, and a hose that is not kinked behind the unit. An ice maker line needs a proper saddle or tee and a supply tube rated for the job. We install each with a shutoff you can reach.

Gas lines for appliances

A gas range, dryer, fireplace or outdoor grill needs a properly sized line with a shutoff, a sediment trap and a pressure test. Gas work is not a do-it-yourself job. Confirm the details with the utility and the inspection agency before work begins.

If you ever smell gas, leave the house and call the utility or 911 from outside. Do not use switches or phones inside.

Water treatment and private systems

Softeners and filters depend on what is in your water. We do not assume a problem, and we suggest testing first. Rockville's city water report does not list hardness, so a test of your own tap decides whether a softener makes sense.

Some properties near Potomac and Derwood use private wells and septic systems, which owners maintain, and performance depends on soil and rock. If you are on one, we describe how a treatment system fits.
