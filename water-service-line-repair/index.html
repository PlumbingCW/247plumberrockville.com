When a service line leak is an emergency

A service line break can send water pouring across a lawn or into a basement wall. Signs include a drop in water pressure, a wet or sunken stretch between the street and the house, the sound of water running when nothing is on and a bill that spikes.

Shut the main valve inside the house. If the leak continues, the break is between the meter and the street or between the meter and the building, and the owner and the utility divide those sections. Our leak detection page explains how we narrow down the location.

While you wait

  • Close the main shutoff inside the house.
  • Note where the ground is wet or sunken.
  • Do not dig by hand near a wet area until the line is located.
  • Check your water bill to confirm whether the City of Rockville or WSSC Water supplies you.

Who owns which part of the line

It depends on your utility. In WSSC Water areas, the pipe from an existing main to the building is the owner's responsibility, and WSSC maintains only the tap. For City of Rockville customers, check the City's guidance on its side of the meter. We recommend confirming this before work so you do not pay for the wrong section.

Signs of a service line problem

  • A wet or sunken stretch of lawn between the street and the house.
  • Water pressure that drops across the whole house.
  • A water bill that rises without an explanation inside the house.
  • Discolored water, especially brown or reddish after the house sits.
  • The sound of running water near the meter when everything is off.

A pressure test, a meter check and a locator tool combine to pinpoint the break, so the excavation is as small as it can be.

Before any digging begins, the line has to be located, and utilities have to mark their lines. We plan that step so the excavation stays small and nothing buried is damaged.

Service lines in Rockville

The City says its service line inventory has found no lead service lines, and that a limited number of galvanized steel lines remain. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside, which causes low pressure and discolored water, so the City also offers free in-home inspections through Public Works if you want to confirm what you have.

Older neighborhoods like Lincoln Park and West End Park have the oldest lines. If a line is galvanized and failing, repiping the house side at the same time can avoid a second excavation. For nearby addresses see our Gaithersburg page.
