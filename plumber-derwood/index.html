A railroad stop that became a suburb

Derwood began as a stop on the B&O Railroad in the 1880s. Lake Needwood opened in 1965, Shady Grove Metro in 1984 and the Intercounty Connector, MD-200, between 2009 and 2011. Housing came in stages as those arrived, which is why a street of older homes can sit beside one of newer townhomes.

The tie to plumbing is the mix. Older houses may still have original drain lines and a water heater replaced two or three times, while newer ones have plastic piping and different issues, mostly clogs and fixture wear. A short camera look tells us which kind of house we are working in.

Near Lake Needwood and the creeks

Low ground near streams and the lake keeps soil wet in some seasons, which can mean damp basements and stressed sump pumps. If your basement takes on water after storms, see sump pump repair first, because a pump failure is the most common cause.

What older and newer Derwood streets have in common

Derwood has older houses and newer townhomes side by side, built in stages as the railroad, the lake and the Metro arrived. What they can share is mature trees, and roots find every weak joint in a sewer lateral. A main line that clogs seasonally is a classic sign.

A yearly camera pass on an older lateral takes little time and shows the roots before they cause a backup. If you do see a backup, stop using water, and call. For a pump that fails during a storm, our sump pump repair page has the steps.

  • Check the cleanout cap and keep it accessible.
  • Keep trees and shrubs from growing over the lateral path where you can.
  • Do not flush wipes, even those sold as flushable.
  • Record when you last cleaned the line, so patterns show.

Ready to call

Because Derwood is so close to our Rockville base, the drive is among the shortest we make. Even so, we do not promise an arrival time we cannot control. Ask what to expect before work begins. If the problem is a flood, close the main first.

Basement moisture near the lake and creeks

Some Derwood lots sit on low, wet ground near Lake Needwood and its feeder streams. A basement there can take on water after heavy rain even if it stayed dry for years. Test your sump pump each spring and check the discharge, and call us if the pit fills faster than it empties.

Where this comes from. According to U.S. Census figures, Derwood has about 2,500 residents. According to WSSC Water's sewer backup guidance, the owner is responsible for the pipe from the property line to the building, and WSSC maintains its main and the connection.
