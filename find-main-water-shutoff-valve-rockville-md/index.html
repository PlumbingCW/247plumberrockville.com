Find and Test Your Main Water Shutoff Valve in Rockville

The main water shutoff is the single most useful valve in your house. When a pipe bursts, it is the difference between a mop and a flood. Most people only find it during an emergency.

This guide shows where it usually is, how to test it and what to do if it will not turn.

Where to look

Follow the water line from the street. In a house with a basement, the main valve is usually on the front wall where the pipe comes in, near the water meter. In a slab house, look in a utility closet, a garage or near the water heater. Some homes also have a second valve outside at the meter.

Mark the valve with a tag and tell everyone in the house. If you cannot find it, trace the cold water pipe from the water heater back toward the wall where it enters. A plumber can locate it quickly, and our burst pipe repair page covers what happens next in an emergency.

Test it before you need it

  1. Turn the valve closed slowly, without forcing it.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point and confirm the flow stops.
  3. Open the valve again fully, then back it off a quarter turn on a gate valve so it does not seize.
  4. Check the packing nut for drips and note any stiffness.

An old gate valve that will not turn needs replacement. Do not force a corroded wheel, because the stem can break and leave you with no control. This is one of the cheapest upgrades in plumbing, and a ball valve is a better choice.

Make a shutoff plan for the house

Knowing where the valve is only helps if everyone in the house knows too. Sit down with family members and show them the main, the water heater valve, the fixture stops and the electric panel. A five-minute walkthrough can save a house from serious damage when a pipe fails while you are away.

Make a simple card with the locations and the plumber's number, and post it inside a utility cabinet. If you travel for long periods, ask whether your plumber recommends closing the main while you are gone, especially in winter.

  • Tag the main valve and the water heater valve.
  • Show every adult and teenager in the house.
  • Keep a flashlight and a valve key near the shutoff.
  • Post the plumber's number inside a cabinet.

Replacing an old gate valve

Gate valves wear out. The gate can corrode in place, the stem can seize and the packing can leak. Replacing one with a full-port ball valve takes a plumber a short time once the water is off at the street or the meter. If the house valve will not close, the utility may need to shut the supply, and we coordinate that.

Seasonal checks that protect the system

Each autumn, walk the house and test the main, the water heater valve and the outdoor spigot shutoff. In late winter, check again after a cold snap, since a frozen pipe may be hiding behind a wall. If you plan a trip, close the main and open a low faucet to drain the lines, then reverse it when you return.

What else to shut off in an emergency

  • The water heater breaker or gas control.
  • Individual stops under sinks and behind toilets.
  • The irrigation system supply.
  • A whole-house filter or softener bypass.

Knowing the fixture stops saves you from shutting the whole house down for a minor leak. The sequence is main for floods, stops for fixtures. If you suspect a hidden leak, leak detection starts with a meter check.

Meters, your supplier and what you may touch

Rockville's water is supplied by the City for most addresses and by WSSC Water for the rest, so your bill shows which utility owns the meter. Homeowners should use the valve on the house side of the meter. The curb stop at the street and the meter itself belong to the utility.

The City also offers free in-home service line inspections through Public Works if you want to confirm what your service line is made of. We do not give the utility's phone numbers here, so use your bill or the city website.
