When a hidden leak is an emergency

A leak you can see is easy. A leak inside a wall or under a slab can run for weeks, soaking drywall, lifting flooring and feeding mold before you notice. Warm spots on a floor, a damp carpet edge, a musty smell or a sudden jump in your water bill are the usual first signs.

If water is spraying or pooling, shut the main valve and call right away. If the signs are quieter, a simple meter check can confirm a leak before anyone opens a wall. Our guide to burst pipe repair covers the fast, visible failures.

A five-minute home leak test

  • Turn off every faucet, the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  • Find your water meter and note whether the dial or low-flow indicator moves.
  • If it moves with everything off, water is leaking somewhere in the house or service line.
  • Take a photo of the meter reading, wait 30 minutes with no use, and photograph it again.

Signs that point to a hidden leak

  • A water bill that rises with no change in how you live.
  • The sound of water running when every fixture is off.
  • A warm spot on a floor, which can mean a hot water line under the slab.
  • Peeling paint, bubbling drywall or a musty smell.
  • Low pressure that develops without a clear cause.

One of these is a reason to check the meter. Two or three together are a reason to call, since the leak is probably active and the damage grows daily.

High bills, meters and slab leaks in Rockville

Rockville's water meters belong to the City for most addresses, and WSSC Water serves the rest, so check your bill to see who reads yours. The City has an advanced metering project underway for City customers that is meant to flag unusual use automatically, though there are no confirmed dates, and WSSC customers are not included. Until a smart meter arrives at your address, your own meter is your best early warning.

Slab leaks matter in any house with supply lines running through or under a concrete floor, and in older homes where copper or galvanized lines were routed that way. A slab leak often shows as a warm floor or a constant sound of water. If the leak is outdoors on the line from the street, see water service line repair and our Aspen Hill page.

Where this comes from. According to the City of Rockville's water meter upgrade project page, the advanced metering project covers City customers only, vendor selection is in progress and WSSC Water customers are not included.
